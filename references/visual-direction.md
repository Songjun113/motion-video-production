# Product-style visual direction

Read before the first storyboard or a visual redesign. View the bundled approved contact sheet as well. The user endorsed its result after rejecting an initial all-dark sequence with repeated slide layouts.

## Design a visual premise

Name the object that carries the story: a document, query, signal, data point, device, interface, or other subject-specific object. Describe the transformations the viewer will follow. Example: an EEG trace enters a decoder; a decoded command activates a robotic finger; repeated commands form a timeline; the timeline resolves into measured results.

For every scene specify one sentence the viewer should retain, the focal object, its scale and location, and the action that explains the sentence. Use a visual metaphor grounded in the mechanism. Placeholders that look like arbitrary dashboards do not demonstrate a product.

## Vary composition across the sequence

Choose complementary shot types rather than repeating a layout:

- A large typographic opening with a floating product or document.
- An orbital or spatial composition for orchestration and relationships.
- An enlarged detail for scanning, comparison, verification, or interaction.
- A convergence shot for a group, output, or result.
- A large metric or brand close with a subordinate comparison.

The default 20-second recipe can use four shots. Change their count, order, and duration to fit the subject. A short opening, one main action per scene, and a stable close are more useful than fitting every detail of a paper.

Keep one shared typographic and material system. Vary background, focal scale, position, angle, and scene density deliberately. A repeated footer or small identity mark can anchor the piece, but must not impose a slide template on every scene.

## Approved palette and sizing example

The reference uses cream `#F5F2E9`, cobalt `#254CED`, mint `#B4F4D3`, pale lilac, charcoal `#172528`, and small coral accents `#FD7956`. Cream establishes the product, cobalt marks active computation, lilac supports close reading, and mint supports the result. This is a reusable preset, not a required palette for every brand.

At 1920 × 1080, the accepted example uses roughly 80-pixel side margins, 70–106-pixel hero copy, 25–38-pixel supporting copy, and one 348-pixel result number. Use fewer words before shrinking text. Scale these proportions for the target frame and inspect at normal viewing size.

Paper and tool surfaces have restrained shadows, rounded corners, and mild tilt, typically 5–13 degrees. Reserve tilt for objects; keep primary reading lines and final numerical comparisons stable. A few recurring accents are enough.

## Motion language

- Stagger related objects so the eye can follow their dependency.
- Use inertial arrival or a small spring overshoot for cards; let text settle promptly.
- Reveal the mechanism: highlight a phrase, move evidence, connect a tool, filter a group, or grow a data mark.
- Carry an outgoing shape or position into the incoming scene. Plan the transformation before implementing a wipe.
- Typical short-form transitions last about 0.4–0.8 seconds, adjusted to the reading load.
- Establish the final value early enough for a stable reading hold. Count-up animations must land on sourced values.
- Tie sound marks to visible action endpoints, with a quiet background bed when appropriate.

## Pre-review gate

Inspect the contact sheet and ask:

1. Can each scene be recognized at thumbnail size by its composition?
2. Is its main message carried by one focal object or text block?
3. Does the progression include an intentional change in scale and attention?
4. Can the outgoing object and incoming object be named at each boundary?
5. Are the actual product or method and its evidence visible?
6. Is the amount of text plausible for the allocated time?

Repair a failed scene before showing the first review. Do not wait for the user to repeat the earlier criticism about darkness or rigid layouts.
